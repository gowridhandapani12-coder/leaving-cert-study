Strand 2 · Networks and graphs

A grid of 0s and 1s.
Multiply it by itself.
Count every route.

An adjacency matrix stores a whole network in a square table that a computer can work with. Write the matrix for a graph and draw the graph from a matrix, multiply matrices by hand, and find out what A² and A³ count: walks, one-stop flights and triangles.

    Labs

    Turn graphs into matrices and back, multiply matrices step by step, and count walks and flights with matrix powers. Every result updates live.

    Graph → matrix

    Tap one vertex, then another, to add or remove the edge between them. Watch the two matching entries of the adjacency matrix change. Tap a vertex once to highlight its row.

    Network
    1Add one edge. How many entries of the matrix change? Why two?
    2Add up the whole matrix. Compare it with the number of edges.
    3Why is every entry on the leading diagonal 0 here?
    Matrix → graph

    Edit mode: tap a cell to switch it between 0 and 1 (its mirror cell changes too) and watch the graph redraw. Challenge mode: you get a mystery matrix. Draw its graph by tapping pairs of vertices, then check.

    Mode
    Challenge
    1In edit mode, make a matrix whose graph is a cycle through all five vertices.
    2Make a row of all 0s. What does that vertex look like on the graph?
    3Solve three challenges in a row. Tick off each 1 above the diagonal as you draw its edge.
    Directed graphs

    A five-person group chat: an arc X → Y means X follows Y. Tap a link to change it: none → one way → the other way → both ways. Rows are "from", columns are "to".

    Arcs
    1Make the matrix symmetric. What do all the arcs have to be?
    2Who has the biggest in-degree (most followers)? Which sum shows it, row or column?
    3Compare the total of the row sums with the total of the column sums.
    Multiply by hand

    Each entry of AB is a row of A times a column of B: multiply in pairs and add. Step through one entry at a time. Then let the computer do a 5 × 5 product and count the work.

    Size
    Steps
    1Work out the next entry on paper before you press the button.
    2Swap to B × A. Is the answer the same? Is it ever?
    3How many multiplications does a 5 × 5 product need? A 100 × 100?
    Powers count walks

    Entry (i, j) of Aⁿ is the number of walks of length n from i to j. Pick a power, then tap a cell of the matrix to list those walks and see the first one on the graph.

    Power
    Collect & fit

    Make random graphs and record the number of edges E, the trace of A² (the sum of its diagonal) and the trace of A³. Fit a line to trace(A²) against E.

    2Record six graphs. What are the gradient and intercept of the line?
    3Compare trace(A³) with the number of triangles. Why 6?
    Two-flight planner

    An illustrative network of direct flights. Tap a "from" airport, then a "to" airport. A counts direct flights, A² counts one-stop journeys, A + A² shows what you can reach within two flights. Tap a route line to cancel it.

    What does A² count here?

    The same matrix algebra answers very different questions.

    Three matrices, three pictures

    Undirected, directed, squared

    Undirected

    Each edge gives two 1s, mirrored across the leading diagonal: the matrix is symmetric.

    Directed

    An arc X → Y puts a 1 in row X, column Y only. Rows give out-degrees, columns give in-degrees.

    Squared

    A² counts walks of length 2. The highlighted 2 means two different two-step walks.

    Top exam tips for this topic

    1. Label the rows and columns of every matrix with the vertex names, in the same order.
    2. For an undirected graph, check your matrix is symmetric and that its entries add up to 2 × edges.
    3. For a directed graph, state your convention: row = from, column = to.
    4. Multiply row by column: cij = ai1b1j + ai2b2j + …. Show at least one entry's working in full.
    5. Remember AB ≠ BA in general: keep the order the question gives.
    6. Interpret in context: (Aⁿ)ij = number of walks of length n from i to j (one-stop flights, friends of friends…).
    7. A walk may repeat vertices, so A² counts walks like P → Q → P on its diagonal.
    8. Past papers for this specification are on examinations.ie. The questions here are practice questions only.
