Strand 3 · Modelling the physical world

Size and direction.
Across and up.
Then gravity wins.

Vectors describe anything with a size and a direction: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force. Split them into i and j parts, add them, multiply them, differentiate them, and use them to predict exactly where a ball will land.

    Drag the vector

    Drag the tip of the displacement vector. Watch its i and j components, its magnitude and its direction change.

    1Make a vector of magnitude 5 that isn't along an axis.
    2Make −3i + 3j. What's its direction? Which quadrant?
    3Make (5, 90°) in polar form. Write it in i, j form.
    Add & scale

    Two successive displacements: a, then b. Drag either tip and see the resultant a + b. Use the slider to scale a.

    1Walk 6 east then 8 north (a = 6i, b = 8j). How far are you from the start?
    2Set k = −1. What does −a look like?
    3Make a + b = 0. What's true about b?
    Dot product

    Drag the tips of a and b. The dot product a·b is a single number: see how it depends on the angle between them.

    1Make a·b = 0. What's the angle?
    2Make a·b negative. What's true about the angle?
    3Point a and b the same way. Compare a·b with |a||b|.
    r → v → a

    A particle follows r(t). Move through time and watch the velocity (the derivative of r) and acceleration (the derivative of v) as arrows.

    Motion
    1Projectile: at what time is v horizontal? What's v·a then?
    2Circle: which way does a point? Compare a with r.
    3Curve: a is constant. Is the path a straight line? Why not?
    Launcher

    Set the launch speed and angle, then launch. Try to land on the flag. Watch the velocity vector: the horizontal part never changes.

    Flight
    1Hit the flag at two different angles with the same speed. How are the angles related?
    2Double u. What happens to the range? To the time of flight?
    3Pause at the top (watch the readout). What's vy there?
    Collect & fit

    A ball launcher fires at an unknown speed. Fire it at different angles, record the range, and plot range against sin 2θ to find the launch speed.

    Experiment
    θ (°)sin 2θRange R (m)

    1Record at least five angles. Is the graph a straight line through the origin?
    2The gradient is u² ÷ g. Use it to work out u.

    Top exam tips for this topic

    1. Write vectors with i and j and keep the parts separate: add i parts to i parts, j parts to j parts.
    2. For a direction, draw a sketch first so you know the quadrant, then use tan⁻¹.
    3. For a projectile, resolve u into u cos θ (across) and u sin θ (up) before anything else.
    4. Horizontal: constant velocity. Vertical: constant acceleration −g. Use v = u + at and s = ut + ½at² on each part.
    5. At the top, the vertical velocity is zero. On landing (horizontal plane), the vertical displacement is zero.
    6. To find the angle between vectors, use a·b = |a||b| cos θ. a·b = 0 means perpendicular.
    7. Differentiate r to get v, and v to get a. Integrate the other way, and use the starting values to find the constants.
    8. State your assumptions (particle, no air resistance, g constant) and give units. Past papers are on examinations.ie; the questions here are practice only.
