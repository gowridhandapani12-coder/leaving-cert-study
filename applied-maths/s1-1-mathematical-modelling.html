Real problem in.
Maths in the middle.

Mathematical modelling is how you use maths on messy, real questions: how long a queue will take, what a ticket should cost, how fast something falls. Learn the cycle that every other strand, and your modelling project, is built on.

    Get exam ready

    Exam prep

    How Applied Maths is assessed, what the question words mean, and unlimited practice with worked solutions.

    Written exam · 80%

    Higher or Ordinary level, 2½ hours, set by the SEC. Covers the three contextual strands (networks, the physical world, a changing world), with modelling throughout.

    Modelling project · 20%

    A written report solving a real problem from a brief the SEC issues each year. Done in 6th year; the same brief for both levels, with a differentiated marking scheme. Sent to the SEC for marking.

    Top exam tips for this strand

    1. Define every variable with its unit: "let t = time in minutes after lunch starts".
    2. State your assumptions and say why each one is needed (it simplifies, or data is missing).
    3. Show the model as an equation before you put numbers in.
    4. Interpret every answer in context: "the queue clears 38 minutes into lunch", not just "37.5".
    5. Check the answer is reasonable: size, sign, units, and does it fit the situation?
    6. To evaluate, compare with real data, test sensitivity, and name a limitation.
    7. To refine, change one assumption, re-solve, and say whether the model improved.
    8. Past papers for this specification are on examinations.ie. The questions here are practice questions only.
