Strand 1 · Exploring the business environment

Who makes
the rules,
and who shapes them?

Irish government policy changes costs and sales in every sector. Laws from the Oireachtas and the EU set the rules. And businesses don't just follow policy: they help to make it. Learn how it all fits together.

Six topics

Three on Irish policy, three on the EU. Each has a visual summary, a lab, flashcards and quiz questions.

From the NCCA specification

Learning outcomes

Tick each one when you're confident. Saved on this device.

Students learn about:

  • how Irish government policy impacts across different sectors of the economy, how policy and legislation differ, and how stakeholders in business can inform and influence policy development (including interest groups and lobbying).
  • the key actors in EU policy development (including the Council of the European Union, the European Commission, the European Parliament, and the European Central Bank).
  • The difference between EU regulations, directives and opinions based on how the Irish government must respond at national level.
  • how the EU impacts on business activity in Ireland with a focus on the purpose of the directive/ legislation, how is has been implemented in Ireland, how it can impact both positively and negatively on different stakeholders, and whether it fosters ethical and sustainable business practice and/or the use of technology.

Note from the specification: the regulation and directive you evaluate must be different to any legislation explored in Strand 4, topics 1 (consumer) and 3 (employee).

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. For each policy, give the sector, what the policy does and how it changes costs, sales or jobs.
  2. Policy is a plan (not legally binding); legislation is law passed by the Oireachtas and enforced by the courts.
  3. Business influences policy through interest groups, lobbying, submissions and consultations. Name real groups: IBEC, ISME, IFA, Chambers Ireland.
  4. Mention the Regulation of Lobbying Act 2015 and the register at lobbying.ie.
  5. EU actors: Commission proposes; Parliament and Council adopt; ECB sets interest rates.
  6. Regulation = applies directly; directive = transposed into Irish law by a deadline; opinion = not binding.
  7. To evaluate an EU law: purpose, how Ireland brought it in, + and − impacts on stakeholders, ethics, sustainability and technology, then a verdict.
  8. Show workings in calculations and put € or % on every answer.
