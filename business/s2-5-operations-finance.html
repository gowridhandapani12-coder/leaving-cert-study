Strand 2 · Understanding enterprise

Who helps you?
What does it cost?
Is there cash in the bank?

Every business runs on two engines. Operations: the key partners, key activities and key resources that make and deliver the product. Finance: the costs it pays, the revenue streams it earns, the sources of finance it uses and the cash flowing in and out. Both change as the business grows, so a good owner watches them and acts early.

Students learn about:

  • the elements of the operational aspect of the business including the key partners a business may have, the key activities of the business and the key resources, while also understanding how these may evolve over time.
  • the financial aspect of a business including the key costs incurred (variable costs, fixed costs) and the potential revenue streams (single transactions or recurring sales) for the business, understanding that these may evolve over time and that the business may need to take action to address issues that arise.

Business model canvas, from the specification's glossary: "A business model canvas is a tool used to map out a business model and includes the key partners, activities and resources, the value proposition (why the customer would choose the product/service), customers, customer relations and channels, costs, and revenues." This topic zooms in on the operations side (partners, activities, resources) and the finance side (costs, revenues) of that canvas. See 2.3 Business planning for the whole canvas.

Match the finance

Sorter

Rule of thumb: match the length of the finance to the life of what it pays for. Day-to-day gaps → short term; equipment and vehicles → medium term; buildings and expansion → long term.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Use the spec's three operational elements: key partners, key activities, key resources. Give an example of each for the business in the question.
  2. "Why may they change over time?" Link each change to a stage of the life cycle, growth, new technology, new customers or new competitors.
  3. Fixed costs stay the same whatever the output (rent, insurance). Variable costs rise with each unit made (raw materials, packaging).
  4. Revenue streams: single transactions (paid once) or recurring sales (paid again and again, such as subscriptions).
  5. Match finance to its use: short term for day-to-day gaps, medium term for equipment and vehicles, long term for buildings and expansion.
  6. In a cash flow question, find the months with a negative balance, name the cause (a big payment, slow receipts, seasonal sales) and match each recommendation to a cause.
  7. Show every formula and working: contribution, break-even, net cash flow, closing balance.
  8. Remember: profit is not cash. A profitable business can still run out of money.
