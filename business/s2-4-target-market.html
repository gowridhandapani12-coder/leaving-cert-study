Strand 2 · Understanding enterprise

Find your customers.
Mix the seven Ps.
Stand out.

Every successful product is aimed at a target market: the group of customers most likely to buy it. Research that market, build a marketing mix that suits it, give customers a reason to choose you (your USP), and use digital tools, ethics, a power-interest grid and a STEEPLE analysis to keep it right.

Learning outcomes

Students learn about:

  • the marketing mix and the importance of identifying the target market; conducting market research and marketing for a business including product/service, pricing, promotion, people, packaging, process, and place.
  • identifying the Unique Selling Point (USP), developing and modifying the marketing mix.
  • how technology changes market research and marketing.
  • the importance of ethics and sustainability in marketing including its influence on marketing companies and on different marketing strategies.
  • the potential of a range of analytical tools to analyse customer interest and the external environment.

*Disruptive impact, from the specification's glossary: "Disruptive impact is often used to described how digital technology can change the way in which businesses operate (including within the workplace) and how consumers interact with business. It can be both positive and negative."

*Power-interest grid: "A power-interest grid is a form of stakeholder mapping which considers the motivations of stakeholders and their ability to exert influence (either positive or negative) thereby mapping them according to their relative power and interest."

*STEEPLE analysis: "A STEEPLE analysis may be used to assess the impact of external factors on a business. It includes consideration of social (S), technological (T), economic (E), environmental (E), political (P), legal (L) and ethical (E) factors."

Ethical and sustainable marketing

You work for an advertising standards body. Check each marketing claim or tactic: is it acceptable, misleading (including greenwashing), or unethical in another way?

Sorter

All the claims are illustrative. Real complaints about adverts in Ireland go to the Advertising Standards Authority (ASA); misleading sales practices can also break consumer protection law.

1What makes "eco-friendly" on its own a weak claim? What evidence would make it acceptable?
2Why are adverts aimed at children treated more strictly?
3How can ethical marketing win customers, not just avoid trouble?

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Learn the spec's seven Ps: product, price, promotion, place, people, packaging, process. Apply each one to the business in the question.
  2. A target market is a specific group of customers. Describe it precisely: age, location, income, lifestyle.
  3. Market research: name the method (survey, interview, focus group, desk research) and say if it is primary or secondary.
  4. A good USP is unique, valued by customers and hard for rivals to copy. "Cheap" is rarely a USP.
  5. "Evaluate and suggest" questions want a judgement on each P, then a specific improvement and why it would help.
  6. Digital: give both sides of the disruptive impact: cheaper, targeted and measurable, but privacy, fierce online competition and fast-changing platforms.
  7. For STEEPLE, write the letter, the factor and its effect on the business (threat or opportunity).
  8. Show the formula and working for numbers: market share, conversion rate, cost per sale, return on a campaign.
