Strand 2 · Understanding enterprise

Enterprise isn't only about starting a company. It happens in schools, hospitals, councils, clubs and your own life. Learn why people start enterprises, what innovators are good at, why innovation matters, and how the Irish government helps new businesses grow.

Learning outcomes

Students learn about:

  • the range of different reasons for starting an enterprise; how enterprise and innovation are present in, and important for, government, society, business, the community, and their own lives.
  • the competencies that support innovation and enterprise development.
  • the role of the Irish government in supporting business and enterprise in Ireland both locally and nationally.

*Competencies (of entrepreneurs), from the specification's glossary: "The integration of knowledge, skills, values and dispositions that support and foster innovation."

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Keep entrepreneur (own venture, own risk) and intrapreneur (enterprising employee, the firm carries the risk) apart, with an example of each.
  2. "Identify examples" means real or local ones: give local, national and international examples if asked.
  3. Reasons for starting up: give a mix of push and pull reasons, applied to the case.
  4. Importance of innovation: cover business, the economy and society, one developed point each.
  5. For each competency: name it, explain it, say why it matters when starting a business.
  6. Learn the spec's definition of competencies: knowledge, skills, values and dispositions that support and foster innovation.
  7. Name the right agency: LEO (small, local, start-ups), Enterprise Ireland (Irish firms that export), IDA Ireland (foreign investment), Údarás na Gaeltachta (Gaeltacht).
  8. Government also helps without grants: low corporation tax, infrastructure, education and training, less red tape.
