Strand 2 · Understanding enterprise

Find a problem.
Shape an idea.
Test before you invest.

Every business starts as an idea, but most ideas need a lot of work before they're worth money. Learn where ideas come from, what helps or blocks them, how design thinking turns a problem into a tested solution, and why a feasibility study comes before the big spend.

Students learn about:

  • how business ideas are developed
  • the significance of design thinking* (including the four phases of clarify, ideate, develop, and implement) to support idea development and the importance of evaluating a business idea to determine the potential future viability of the business.

*Design thinking, from the specification's glossary: "An innovative, non-linear approach to the development of products and services which focuses on solutions for the end user."

Design thinking project: the canteen queue

Students lose 15 minutes of a 40-minute lunch queuing in the canteen. Guide the student council through the four phases. Choose the best action at each step; watch for the moment the process loops back.

An illustrative school project. The same four phases work for a product, a service or a public service.

1Where in the project was it person-centred? Where was it solution-centred?
2Why was a paper prototype better than building an app first?
3The project went back a phase. Is that a failure? Why not?

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Sources of ideas: say whether each is internal or external, and apply it to the case.
  2. "Determine the factors": name the factor, explain how it helps or hinders the idea, and link it to the business in the question.
  3. Learn the four phases in order: clarify, ideate, develop, implement, with one action for each.
  4. Iterative means you loop back and improve after testing. Person-centred means you start with the user's needs. Solution-centred means you keep working towards something that solves the problem.
  5. Learn the spec's definition of design thinking: an innovative, non-linear approach to developing products and services which focuses on solutions for the end user.
  6. A feasibility study covers market, technical, financial, legal and environmental/ethical questions. Say why each matters before money is committed.
  7. Break-even = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit. Show the formula and units.
  8. "Appreciate the importance" questions want benefits: reduces risk, attracts finance, saves money and time, finds problems early.
