Strand 2 · Understanding enterprise

Write the plan.
Map the model.
Capture the value.

A business plan shows where a business is going and how it will get there. At its heart is the business model: how the business creates value for customers, delivers it, and captures some of it as income. Learn the plan's functions, plan with ethics and sustainability in mind, fill in a business model canvas and compare traditional and digital models.

Learning outcomes

Students learn about:

  • the importance of a coherent business plan at different stages of business development and how it supports business start-up, operations, funding and development, and the need to consider both ethical and sustainability factors.
  • the role of business models* in generating income and creating, delivering, and capturing value for both the enterprise and its customers.
  • the importance of the business model and the elements of the business model canvas*.
  • a range of business models including retail, manufacturing, subscription, franchise, and affiliate.
  • the digital economy including marketplace, subscription, crowdfunding and advertising-supported models; the similarities and differences when compared to non-digital business models, and how businesses have adapted to or developed based on the digital economy and other internal or external drivers.

*Business model, from the specification's glossary: "A business model is an element within the business plan which outlines how a company will operate, create, deliver, and capture value in economic, social, and cultural contexts."

*Business model canvas: "A business model canvas is a tool used to map out a business model and includes the key partners, activities and resources, the value proposition (why the customer would choose the product/service), customers, customer relations and channels, costs, and revenues."

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Functions of a business plan: give start-up, funding, operations and development, and say who uses the plan for each.
  2. "Appreciate the importance" questions want benefits: clear goals, finance, a benchmark to measure against, spotting problems early.
  3. For ethics and sustainability, give a specific planning decision (suppliers, pay, packaging, energy) and its benefit to the business.
  4. Learn the spec's definition: a business model shows how a company will operate, create, deliver and capture value.
  5. Name all nine blocks of the canvas, with the value proposition in the centre.
  6. "Compare" means similarities and differences: write about both, ideally in a table.
  7. Digital models: marketplace, subscription, crowdfunding, advertising-supported. For each say who pays, for what, and what it needs (users, data, trust).
  8. Show the formula and working for numbers: commission = sales × %, monthly revenue = subscribers × fee.
