Strand 2 · Understanding enterprise

What could go wrong?
How likely? How bad?
What will you do about it?

Every enterprise takes risks: money can run out, customers can stay away, equipment can break and people can get hurt. You can't remove all risk, but you can manage it. Spot the risks, rate how likely and how serious each one is, then avoid it, spread it, prevent it or insure against it.

Risks of enterprise

Aoife is starting Tonnta Kayaks (illustrative), a kayak and paddleboard hire business near Clifden. She has put her savings and a bank loan into it. Sort each challenge she faces into a type of risk. The columns fill as you go.

Sorter

1Which risks could Aoife lose most money on? Which are most likely?
2Which risks are personal to Aoife as an entrepreneur, not just to the business?
3Which risks can she control, and which come from outside?
Risk matrix

Rate each of Tonnta's risks for likelihood (how likely it is to happen) and impact (how bad it would be), from 1 to 5. Risk score = likelihood × impact. The matrix shows which risks to deal with first.

Risk
Ratings

The suggested ratings are a starting point for discussion. A real risk register is reviewed regularly, because risks change as the business grows.

1Which risk has the highest score? Is it the most likely one?
2Find a risk that is unlikely but would be a disaster. Why can't Aoife ignore it?
3How would hiring qualified instructors change the rating of a customer injury?
Choose a strategy

For each risk, choose the best way to manage it: avoid it (don't do the risky thing), spread it (don't depend on one thing), prevent it (make it less likely or less serious) or insure against it (pay a premium so an insurer covers the loss).

Sorter

The zones are a rule of thumb, not a law: most businesses combine strategies, for example preventing fires and insuring the building.

1Which strategy suits risks that are unlikely but very costly?
2Why can't Aoife avoid every risk? What would she give up?
3Find a risk where two strategies together work better than one.
Spread the risk

Bad weather can wipe out a kayak season. Aoife can spread her risk by adding other income streams (school groups, a café, online sales). Pick the number of streams and how closely they are linked, then run 40 seasons. Collect a result for each number of streams and see the graph.

Seasons

Simulated data. Every stream is expected to earn the same in total (€60 000 a year between them); only how much it swings from year to year changes. "Spread" is the standard deviation of yearly profit.

1Record a result for 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 streams. How does the number of bad years change?
2Clear, set "linked" to 0.8 (all streams depend on the weather) and repeat. What happens to the benefit of spreading?
3What real choices would make Tonnta's streams less linked?
Prevention on a budget

Each risk has a chance of happening this year and a cost if it does. Expected loss = probability × cost. Switch on prevention measures. Each costs money every year but makes a risk less likely. Find the plan with the lowest total cost of risk.

Prevention measures (tap to switch on or off)

Figures are illustrative. Expected loss is an average over many years: in most years nothing happens, and in a bad year the loss is far bigger. That's why the big, rare risks are also insured.

1Which measure saves more than it costs by the widest margin?
2Switch on the security guard. Why does the total cost of risk go up?
3Fire is rare and prevention only halves it. What else should Aoife do about fire?
Insurance

A storm floods Tonnta's boathouse. Set the true value of the contents, the sum insured on the policy, the loss and the excess. See how much the insurer pays and how much Aoife pays herself. Then match each risk to the right insurance product.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Risk = the chance that something goes wrong and the business loses money or fails. Name the type: financial, market, operational, people, external (legal, economic, weather).
  2. Challenges for an entrepreneur include personal risks too: losing savings, stress, long hours, a secure job given up.
  3. Assessing risk = likelihood × impact. Say which risks come first and why.
  4. The spec's strategies: avoidance, spread, prevention and choosing suitable insurance. For "analyse", give the strategy, apply it to the business, and weigh its cost and limits.
  5. Insurance: name the product (public liability, employer's liability, property, motor, business interruption, cyber, product liability, key person) and the risk it covers.
  6. Know the words premium, excess, sum insured, under-insurance and average clause; and the principles of utmost good faith, insurable interest and indemnity.
  7. Not every risk can be insured: a business can't insure against making a loss or a rival opening next door. Those risks are managed by planning, spreading and adapting.
  8. Show every formula and working: risk score, expected loss, premium, compensation.
