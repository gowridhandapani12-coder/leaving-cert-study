The Olympiad.

The Irish Mathematical Olympiad (IrMO) is Ireland's national problem-solving contest and the road to the IMO and EGMO teams. Learn how the year works, the four topic areas (algebra, number theory, geometry, combinatorics), the key techniques, and try real past problems with hints and full worked solutions.

    AM ≥ GM ≥ HM

    Two positive numbers a and b. The semicircle shows the arithmetic, geometric, harmonic and quadratic means as lengths. When are they equal?

    1Make the means equal. What must be true of a and b?
    2Why is GM the half-chord? (Hint: similar triangles / intersecting chords.)
    3Use AM ≥ GM to show x + 1/x ≥ 2 for x > 0.
    Collect & fit

    Count the primes up to x (a sieve does it live). Record several x values, then plot π(x) against x/ln x and fit a line.

    Experiment
    xπ(x)x / ln xratio

    1Record x = 100, 1 000, 10 000 and 100 000. Does the ratio approach 1?
    2The Prime Number Theorem says π(x) ~ x/ln x. What does "~" mean here?
    3Why are there infinitely many primes? (Euclid: look at p₁p₂…pₙ + 1.)
    Angle chasing

    Drag vertex A. The bisectors from B and C meet at the incentre I and cut the sides at D and E. Watch ∠BIC = 90° + A/2, and see when IE = ID (IrMO 2009).

    1Check ∠BIC = 90° + ½∠A for several shapes.
    2Make ∠A = 60°. What happens to IE and ID?
    3When ∠A = 60°, why is AEID cyclic?

    About 36 minutes per problem

    IrMO papers have historically had two papers of three hours, five problems each (e.g. IrMO 2009). Check irmo.ie for the current format.

    IMO and EGMO

    Two days, three problems a day in 4½ hours. Each problem is worth 7 points.

    How to attack an olympiad problem

    1. Try small cases and draw a big, accurate diagram. Patterns come from data.
    2. Guess the answer before proving it, then aim the proof at the guess.
    3. Look for invariants, extremes (the smallest counterexample) and symmetry.
    4. Work backwards: what would you need to show just before the end?
    5. For "find all", prove both directions: your answers work, and nothing else does.
    6. Write a complete proof: define everything, justify every step, state where you used each condition.
    7. Partial progress counts: write up special cases and key lemmas if you can't finish.
    8. Problems here are real past problems with our own solutions, not official marking schemes.
