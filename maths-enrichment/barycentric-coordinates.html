Maths Enrichment · Olympiad geometry

Put masses on a triangle.
Find the balance point.
That's a coordinate.

Barycentric coordinates describe every point in the plane of a triangle by three numbers: masses at the vertices, or ratios of areas. Triangle centres get beautiful formulas, collinearity becomes a determinant, and some hard olympiad geometry problems turn into algebra.

    Put masses x, y, z at A, B, C. The balance point P = (x : y : z). Watch the cevians and the ratios they cut on the sides.

    Areas = coordinates

    Drag P anywhere. The three coloured triangles PBC, PCA, PAB have (signed) areas in the ratio x : y : z.

    1Drag P to make all three areas equal. Which point is that?
    2Drag P outside the triangle across BC. Which coordinate turns negative?

    Drag the vertices. The centres are computed from their barycentric formulas. Check the Euler line and the Nagel line.

    Collinearity test

    Drag P, Q and R. They are collinear exactly when the determinant of their barycentric coordinates is 0.

    Points
    1Drag R onto line PQ. Watch the determinant go to 0.
    2Put R at the midpoint of PQ. Is R = (P + Q)/2 in normalised coordinates?
    3Why must the coordinates be normalised for the ratio k = PR/PQ?
    Collect & fit

    Ceva's theorem

    Choose the ratios BD/DC, CE/EA and AF/FB. The cevians meet in one point exactly when the product is 1.

    How to bash a problem with barycentrics

    1. Choose the reference triangle so the important points have simple coordinates (often the given triangle ABC).
    2. Write known points first: vertices (1,0,0), midpoints, feet of cevians from ratios, centres from the table.
    3. A point on line BC has x = 0. A point dividing a segment in a ratio is a weighted average of normalised endpoints.
    4. Use the determinant for collinearity, and for areas once the points are normalised.
    5. Keep coordinates homogeneous (with colons) while multiplying through; normalise only when you need areas or ratios.
    6. Use a·cos A = (b² + c² − a²)a/(2bc) style identities to turn trigonometry into side lengths.
    7. Check special cases (equilateral, right-angled) to catch algebra slips.
    8. In a written olympiad solution, explain each coordinate you use. A bash is valid but must be complete and readable.

    Key formulas to memorise

    1. G = (1 : 1 : 1), I = (a : b : c)
    2. Na = (s − a : s − b : s − c), Sp = (b + c : c + a : a + b)
    3. O = (a²(b² + c² − a²) : …), H = ((a² + b² − c²)(a² − b² + c²) : …)
    4. Traces of (x : y : z): BD/DC = z/y, CE/EA = x/z, AF/FB = y/x
    5. Collinear ⇔ det = 0; [PQR]/[ABC] = det (normalised)
    6. Isogonal conjugate of (x : y : z) is (a²/x : b²/y : c²/z)
