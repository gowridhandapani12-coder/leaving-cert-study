Topaic 3 · Topic 3

Gaeilge
timpeall orainn.
Irish all around us.

Irish on signs, on TG4 and the radio, in the Gaeltacht, in debates about identity, and next to other languages. Learn the phrases, master the grammar that catches people out (trí huaire, don triúr againn, i gConamara, níos fusa ná…), and practise answers you can actually say.

Spriocanna · Goals

Quoted from the syllabus (Topic 3), with an English summary. Tick each one when you're confident. Saved on this device.

"Ba chóir a oiread agus is féidir den teanga bheo atá i dtimpeallacht na scoláirí a úsáid chun iad a spreagadh chun foghlama." Use as much as possible of the living Irish around you (broadcasting and other resources) to learn, and to understand Irish culture better.

    The syllabus groups this topic into: 3.1 An Ghaeilge Timpeall Orainn (Irish around us) · 3.2 An Ghaeilge ar na meáin (Irish in the media) · 3.3 An Ghaeilge agus Féiniúlacht (Irish and identity) · 3.4 An Ghaeltacht · 3.5 An Ghaeilge agus teangacha eile (Irish and other languages). At Higher Level, "An Ghaeilge" is a topic of its own, with sub-topics such as Athbheochan na Gaeilge, an dátheangachas, Éireannaigh nua agus an Ghaeilge, TG4 and the Irish-language radio stations. Example phrases on this page marked "siollabas" come straight from the syllabus.

    Comharthaí

    Irish is on signs all around you. Read the sign and pick what it means. Each sign you get right goes up on the street.

    Cad is brí leis? (what does it mean?)
    Comharthaí
    1Read all the signs. Which ones have you seen on your own street or in your town?
    2Look at "Oifig an Phoist". Why is it "Phoist" and not "Post"?
    3Say out loud where you'd see each sign: "Feicim é ag an…".
    Na meáin

    Log the Irish you watch and hear each week. Then survey your class: does more listening go with more confidence in the oral? The line of best fit updates as you ask more students.

    Suirbhé ranga (class survey)
    1Move TG4 from 2 to 3 hours. Why does "uair" become "huaire"?
    2Ask 30 students. Is the slope of the line positive? Does that prove listening causes confidence?
    3Pick a programme type you hate and say why, out loud, using "Tá an ghráin dhearg agam ar…".
    Díospóireacht

    The motion: "Ba cheart an Ghaeilge a bheith éigeantach don Ardteist." Sort the twelve arguments into for (ar son) and against (in aghaidh). Then pick your side and the lab writes the start of your speech.

    Sorter
    1Sort all twelve. Which syllabus phrase is the strongest argument against?
    2Slide your stand from one end to the other. Which phrases change, and which tense is "Bheinn"?
    3Argue the side you disagree with for 30 seconds. It's great debate practice.
    An Ghaeltacht

    Pick a Gaeltacht area on the map. See where it is, which dialect is spoken there, and how to say "in" that place. Then book somewhere to stay.

    Ceantar Gaeltachta (Gaeltacht area)
    1Click Conamara, then Corca Dhuibhne. How does "How are you?" change between dialects?
    2Change the number of people from 2 to 3. Why is it "triúr" and not "trí"?
    3Slide the nights to 7. What happens to "oíche"?
    Teangacha eile

    Irish is a Celtic language. Pick a word and see it in its sister languages: watch the c in Irish turn into a p in Welsh and Breton. Then compare Irish with a language you study.

    1Look at "ceann", "cúig" and "ceathair". What sound do Welsh and Breton have instead of the c?
    2Compare Irish with French: "níos fusa" or "níos deacra"? Give a reason in Irish.
    3Which word looks the same in all four languages?
    An comhrá

    Pick an oral exam question about Irish, the Gaeltacht or the media and build your answer from the phrases. The meters show length, range of tenses and linking words: a rough guide, not official marks.

    Ceist (question)
    1Build an answer that uses at least three tenses.
    2Say it aloud and time it. Aim for about 30 seconds per question.
    3Swap in your own details: your Gaeltacht trip, your favourite programme, your view.

    Exam prep

    How Irish is assessed, how this topic comes up, and practice with feedback.

    An Bhéaltriail (oral) · 240 marks, 40%

    Common to Higher and Ordinary Level, around Easter of 6th year. Examiners often ask about Irish itself, the Gaeltacht and TG4.

    Páipéar 1 · 160 marks, 27%

    Cluastuiscint (listening) 60 + Ceapadóireacht (writing) 100. The listening uses real speakers from all three dialects.

    Páipéar 2 · 200 marks, 33%

    HL: two reading comprehensions 50 + 50, prose 30, poetry 30, extra literature 40. (OL: prose 50, poetry 50.)

    Top tips for this topic

    1. Have a Gaeltacht story ready in the past tense: where, how long (trí seachtaine), who you stayed with, what you did. If you've never been, say where you'd like to go, in the conditional.
    2. Name real Irish media: TG4, Ros na Rún, Raidió na Gaeltachta, Raidió na Life, and say what kind of programme it is: "Is clár cúrsaí reatha é" (siollabas).
    3. Opinions: Is dóigh liom / Measaim / Is é mo bharúil gur… (siollabas). Strong views: "Bheinn féin go láidir i bhfábhar na teanga" (siollabas).
    4. Times and hours: uair amháin, dhá uair, trí huaire, seacht n-uaire. Add "an chloig" for hours.
    5. Places: i + urú: i gConamara, i nGaillimh, i mBaile Átha Cliath (siollabas); in before a vowel: in Árainn.
    6. Comparing languages: níos fusa / níos deacra / níos casta ná… (siollabas).
    7. If you don't understand the examiner, use the syllabus phrases: "Arís, le do thoil." "Ní thuigim céard is brí le…"
    8. The tasks here are practice tasks, not official SEC papers.
