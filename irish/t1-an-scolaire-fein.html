    Build your family. The tree and the Irish sentences update as you go, with the right number words.

    Treoracha

    You're at home. Pick a place in town and get directions in Irish, step by step, along the route on the map.

    Cá bhfuil…? (Where is…?)
    1Get directions to three places. Which phrases repeat?
    2Give a friend directions from your real house to your school, in Irish.
    3Describe your area: what's there, and what's missing?
    Tréithe

    Sort the qualities into good and bad. Then build a sentence about a friend, with the right pronoun and "an-".

    My friend is…
    Sorter
    1Sort all twelve. Which ones did you not know?
    2Look at the sentence: which words change after "an-"? Which don't?
    3Describe your best friend with three qualities.
    Suirbhé · Survey

    Survey your year group about their favourite pastime. Ask ten students at a time and watch the percentages settle as the sample grows.

    Suirbhé
    1After 10 students, which pastime looks most popular? After 200?
    2Why do the lines wobble at first and then flatten out?
    3Report the result in Irish using "Taitníonn … le …".
    Buiséad

    You have a part-time job. Set your pay and hours, decide how to spend it, and see what's left to save.

    1How many hours do you need to work to save €50 a week?
    2Overspend on purpose. What does the Irish say now?
    3Answer out loud: "Cad a dhéanann tú le d'airgead?"

    Exam prep

    How Irish is assessed, how this topic comes up, and practice with feedback.

    An Bhéaltriail (oral) · 240 marks, 40%

    Common to Higher and Ordinary Level, around Easter of 6th year. This topic is the heart of the comhrá (conversation).

    Páipéar 1 · 160 marks, 27%

    Cluastuiscint (listening) 60 + Ceapadóireacht (writing) 100.

    Páipéar 2 · 200 marks, 33%

    HL: two reading comprehensions 50 + 50, prose 30, poetry 30, extra literature 40. (OL: prose 50, poetry 50.)

    Top tips for this topic

    1. Prepare, don't memorise word for word. Learn chunks you can recombine for any question about you.
    2. Go beyond one sentence: answer, add a detail, add an opinion, add a reason ("mar…").
    3. Use several tenses: what you do (láithreach), did (caite), will do (fáistineach), would do (coinníollach).
    4. Get the numbers right: personal numbers for people, "bliana/mbliana" for ages.
    5. Watch mo + lenition (mo mháthair) and m' before a vowel (m'athair).
    6. Use é/í correctly: "Is duine cineálta í" for a girl or woman.
    7. In the oral, keep talking: if you don't know a word, describe it another way.
    8. The tasks here are practice tasks, not official SEC papers.
