Question A asks you to understand a text, back up your points with evidence and explain how the writer's language works. Practise on three short public-domain extracts: annotate them, sort their techniques, track their tone, measure their sentences, map their genres and plan a full answer.

    Exam prep

    Where Question A sits in the exam, how answers are marked, and practice with feedback.

    Question A · 50 marks

    Comprehension questions on one of the three texts in Section I. In recent papers it has usually had three parts; check the marks printed beside each part.

    Rest of Paper 1 · 150 marks

    Question B (50, functional writing on a different text) and a composition (100) in Section II.

    Paper 2 · 200 marks

    Single text 60, comparative study 70, unseen poem 20, prescribed poetry 50.

    Top exam tips for Question A

    1. Read all three texts' questions first, then pick the Question A you can answer best. Question B must be on a different text.
    2. Spend about 45 minutes: roughly one minute per mark, plus reading time.
    3. Answer in your first sentence, using the question's key words.
    4. One point per paragraph: point, short quotation, explanation.
    5. Match the number of points to the marks and the question ("three reasons", "four features").
    6. For style questions, name the technique and say what it does to the reader.
    7. Use your own words when asked to explain; quote when asked to support.
    8. Proofread names and quotations. Questions here are practice questions, not official SEC papers.
