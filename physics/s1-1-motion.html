Strand 1 · Section 1.1 · Particle motion in a straight line

Where is it?
How fast?
Speeding up?

Every journey in a straight line can be described with four quantities: displacement, velocity, acceleration and time. Learn to read motion graphs, derive and use the three equations of motion, and add vectors in one and two dimensions.

Learning outcomes

Students learn about:

  • basic concepts for describing the motion of a particle; displacement, velocity, acceleration and time.
  • relationships between the concepts; v = Δs/Δt, a = Δv/Δt
  • graphical representation and interpretation: displacement-time graphs, velocity-time graphs
  • the kinematics equations under constant acceleration: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as
  • identifying and representing scalar and vector quantities
  • resolving vectors into perpendicular components
  • calculating the resultant of two vectors

Modelling motion · LO 1.1.1

Six graph shapes to know

The same motion looks different on a displacement–time and a velocity–time graph. Learn to read both.

Three big pictures

Slopes, areas and components

Area = displacement

The area under a velocity–time graph is the displacement. Its slope is the acceleration.

Components

Fx = F cos θ and Fy = F sin θ. Together they replace the original vector.

Adding vectors

3 N and 4 N at right angles add to 5 N, not 7 N. Direction matters.

Secondary data · LO 1.1.2

Varying motion: a 100 m world record

Split times every 10 m for Usain Bolt's 9.58 s run (Berlin, 2009), as widely published from the race's biomechanics analysis. Average speed for each 10 m = 10 m ÷ time for that 10 m.

The first 10 m includes the reaction time at the start (about 0.15 s). He accelerates for about 60 m, reaches a top speed of about 12.3 m/s, then slows slightly. Check the figures against another source before using them in an investigation.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Displacement, velocity and acceleration are vectors: give a size and a direction (or a sign).
  2. List s, u, v, a, t, tick the three you know, then pick the equation without the one you don't need.
  3. "From rest" means u = 0. "Comes to rest" means v = 0. Slowing down means a is negative.
  4. Convert km/h to m/s first: ÷ 3.6.
  5. s–t graph: slope = velocity. v–t graph: slope = acceleration, area = displacement.
  6. To derive the equations, start from a = (v − u)/t and the area under the v–t graph.
  7. Resolving: F cos θ is the component next to the angle, F sin θ the one opposite it.
  8. Resultant of perpendicular vectors: Pythagoras for size, tan⁻¹ for direction. Always give the angle.
