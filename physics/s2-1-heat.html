Strand 2 · Section 2.1 · Heat energy and temperature change

Put heat in.
Does it get hotter?
Not always.

Heat energy can raise the temperature of a substance or change its state. Learn how thermometers work, why water takes so long to heat, why ice melts at a steady 0 °C, and how insulation and heat pumps save energy.

Particle models

How heat moves and changes things

Conduction

Hot particles vibrate more and pass energy to their neighbours. Free electrons make metals fast.

Convection

Warm fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises. Cooler fluid sinks to take its place.

Changes of state

While melting or boiling, energy breaks bonds instead of raising the temperature.

Secondary data · LO 2.1.2

Specific heat capacities

Energy needed to warm 1 kg by 1 K, in J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ (typical published values). Water's is huge, which is why the sea warms slowly and makes a good store of heat.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Write the formula first, then substitute, then the answer with its unit.
  2. A temperature change is the same in K and °C: Δθ = 15 °C = 15 K. Add 273 only for a temperature, not a change.
  3. Q = mcΔθ when the temperature changes; Q = ml when the state changes. Use both if both happen.
  4. Mass in kg: 250 g = 0.250 kg. Energy in J: 2.5 kJ = 2500 J.
  5. Electrical heating: Q = Pt = VIt, with t in seconds.
  6. Method of mixtures: heat lost = heat gained. Write one side for each body.
  7. A thermometer needs a thermometric property that changes measurably with temperature, plus two fixed points.
  8. U-value: rate of heat transfer = U × A × Δθ. Lower U, better insulator.
