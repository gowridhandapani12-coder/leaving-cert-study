Students learn about:

  • Newton’s 3 laws of motion
  • the concepts of mass, centre of mass and a force as a vector quantity
  • types of forces: Normal, Frictional, Resistant, Tension, Buoyancy, Gravitational
  • resultant force as the sum of all forces: Fnet = ma
  • pressure: P = F/A; in fluids P = hρg
  • the concept of density: ρ = m/V
  • the concept of momentum: p = mv
  • collisions as governed by Newton’s laws of motion and by conservation of momentum

Builds on 1.1 (vectors, components and the equations of motion). Springs and Hooke's law come next, in 1.3.

Modelling with Newton's laws · LO 1.2.1

Six free-body diagrams to know

Draw the object as a dot or box, then add every force acting on it. Equal and opposite arrows mean no resultant force.

Three big pictures

F = ma, pressure and momentum

a ∝ F

Double the resultant force, double the acceleration. A heavier object gives a less steep line (slope = 1/m).

Deeper = more pressure

P = hρg: pressure in a liquid rises in a straight line with depth. Denser liquid, steeper line.

Momentum before = after

In any collision with no outside force, the total momentum stays the same. Kinetic energy may not.

Secondary data · LO 1.2.6 and 1.2.7

A collision seen by two force sensors

Sample data-logger results (simulated, for practice): glider A (0.50 kg, 0.80 m/s) hits glider B (0.50 kg, at rest) with spring buffers. A force sensor on each glider records the force during the 0.10 s contact.

The two traces are mirror images: at every instant the force on A equals the force on B in size and is opposite in direction (Newton's third law). Each area is about 0.39 N s, the impulse, which equals each glider's change in momentum (Newton's second law). So A loses exactly the momentum B gains.

Four sample air-track runs. Velocities to the right are positive. Total momentum before and after agrees within about 3%, within the experimental error.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. Start every force problem with a free-body diagram: one object, every force on it, labelled arrows.
  2. F in F = ma is the resultant force: (forward forces) − (backward forces).
  3. Weight is a force: W = mg, in newtons. Mass is in kilograms and doesn't change.
  4. Newton's third law pairs act on different objects, so they never cancel each other.
  5. Pressure: convert cm² to m² (÷ 10 000) and g/cm³ to kg/m³ (× 1000).
  6. Momentum is a vector: pick a positive direction and give signs to velocities going the other way.
  7. Momentum is conserved in every collision; kinetic energy only in elastic ones.
  8. In 2D, conserve momentum separately along and across the original direction.
