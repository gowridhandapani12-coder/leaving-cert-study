Strand 2 · Section 2.2 · Travelling waves

The wave moves on.
The rope stays put.
Energy travels.

A wave is a disturbance that carries energy from place to place without carrying matter. Learn the two kinds of wave, the words that describe them, the wave equation v = fλ, why bigger waves carry more energy, and what happens at resonance.

Students learn about: travelling waves as models of disturbances transferring energy without transferring matter

  • mechanical/electromagnetic waves longitudinal/ transverse waves
  • radiation
  • wave characteristics: rest position, displacement, trough, crest, amplitude, wavelength, velocity, time period, frequency
  • the relationship between amplitude and energy carried by a wave
  • resonance as the observed phenomenon when driving frequency = the natural frequency of vibration

Equations for this section: T = 1/f, v = fλ and c = fλ. The electromagnetic spectrum is in 2.4, sound in 2.5 and stationary waves in 2.6.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. A wave transfers energy, not matter. Say it in those words.
  2. Transverse vs longitudinal: compare the direction of vibration with the direction of travel.
  3. Mechanical waves need a medium; electromagnetic waves don't.
  4. Amplitude is measured from the rest position to a crest, not crest to trough.
  5. Write v = fλ, substitute, then answer with a unit. Convert cm to m and MHz to Hz first.
  6. T = 1/f: a 50 Hz wave has a period of 0.02 s.
  7. Energy ∝ A²: double the amplitude, four times the energy.
  8. Resonance: driving frequency = natural frequency, so the amplitude becomes large. Give a named real example.
