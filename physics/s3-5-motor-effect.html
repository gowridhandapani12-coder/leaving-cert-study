Strand 3 · Section 3.5 · Force on a current-carrying conductor

Push a current.
Feel the kick.
Spin a motor.

Put a wire carrying a current in a magnetic field and it feels a force. Measure that force on a balance, see where it comes from in the combined "catapult" field, find its direction with Fleming's left-hand rule, turn a coil, keep it spinning with a split-ring commutator, and find the motor effect in loudspeakers, fans and electric cars.

Students learn about:

  • The force experienced by a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.
  • Fleming's left-hand rule/right-hand rule to determine the direction of a motor effect force

Equation in the specification for 3.5: F = BIL. Builds on 3.4 (magnetic fields, F = Bqv) and 3.3 (current); leads on to 3.6 (induction and the generator). The extra relationships on this page (F = BIL sin θ, F = NBIL, turning effect of a coil) are used to model the motor effect and the DC motor.

Experiment: F = BIL on a balance

A U-shaped magnet sits on a top-pan balance. A stiff wire, held by a clamp, runs through the gap between its poles (you see the wire end on). When a current flows, the wire is pushed one way and the magnet the other way, so the balance reading changes. Choose what to vary, take readings and plot the force against it.

Fleming's left-hand rule at any angle

Looking down on a wire lying between two flat magnet poles. Turn the wire to any angle θ to the field and set the current. The force is at right angles to both the wire and the field, so here it points into or out of the page. Its size depends on the angle.

Current flows
Field

B = 0.20 T, length of wire in the field L = 10 cm. F = BIL when the wire is at right angles to the field; at other angles only the part of the wire across the field counts, F = BIL sin θ. The spec equation is F = BIL (wire at right angles).

1At what angle is the force biggest? When is it zero?
2Use your left hand: first finger along the field, second finger along the current. Does your thumb agree with the picture?
3Turn θ past 90°. Does the force change direction? Past 180°?
Forces on a coil

A rectangular coil between magnet poles, seen end on: you see the two long sides of the coil as • and ×. The current flows opposite ways in the two sides, so the forces are opposite: together they turn the coil. Set the angle, then let go and watch what happens without a commutator.

Coil sides 5.0 cm long in the field, coil 4.0 cm wide. Force on each side F = NBIL. Turning effect (moment of the couple) = F × width × cos(angle), largest when the plane of the coil is along the field and zero when it is at right angles to the field. The animation is slowed down.

1At what angle is the turning effect biggest? At what angle is it zero?
2Press Let go. Why does the coil rock and then stop with its plane at right angles to the field?
3Does the force on each side change as the coil turns? What does change?
Model a DC motor

The coil is connected to a dc supply through a split-ring commutator and two carbon brushes. Every half turn the commutator swaps the connections, so the current in the coil reverses and the coil keeps turning the same way. Swap to slip rings to see why the commutator is needed.

Supply
Connections

Simplified model: the turning effect is proportional to NBI and to cos(angle); friction and air resistance limit the speed. The rpm figure is a model value for a small hobby motor; the animation is slowed down. Real motors use many coils at different angles and curved poles, so the turning effect is smoother.

1Watch the brushes. At which position of the coil does the current reverse?
2Switch to slip rings. Why does the coil stop instead of going round?
3Give three ways to make the motor turn faster. How do you make it turn the other way?
The motor effect at work: a loudspeaker

A cut-through moving-coil loudspeaker. A light coil sits in the gap of a permanent magnet and is fixed to a paper cone. An alternating current in the coil makes the force on it change direction, so the cone moves in and out and makes a sound wave.

Top exam tips for this chapter

  1. F = BIL: B in tesla, I in amperes, L in metres. Convert cm to m first.
  2. The force is biggest when the wire is at right angles to the field and zero when it is parallel to the field.
  3. Fleming's left-hand rule: First finger Field (N to S), seCond finger Current (+ to −), thuMb Motion (force).
  4. Reverse the current or the field and the force reverses. Reverse both and it stays the same.
  5. The motor effect comes from two fields interacting: the wire is pushed from the strong side of the combined field to the weak side.
  6. On a coil, the forces on the two sides are equal and opposite but not in line, so they turn the coil.
  7. The split-ring commutator reverses the current in the coil every half turn, when the coil is at right angles to the field, so it keeps turning the same way.
  8. Faster motor: more current, stronger magnet, more turns (and a soft iron core). To reverse it: reverse the current or the magnet.
